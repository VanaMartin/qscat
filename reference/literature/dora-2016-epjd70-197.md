## Why this repository cares

The paper compares several electronic target and scattering models for CO.
Its model-dependent low-energy resonance values and explicit discussion of
SEP imbalance prevent the CO pilot's apparent agreement with a familiar
resonance energy from being mistaken for convergence.

## Findings and limits

The main study concerns higher-lying resonances, not a certified error bar for
the lowest shape resonance (p. 1). Table 4 shows substantial dependence on
basis, active space and number of target states (p. 6). SEP may also produce
pseudoresonances because its polarization configurations do not introduce
the corresponding explicit excited target channels (p. 5). Numerical
agreement of two fitting methods checks extraction, rather than electronic
model completeness (pp. 6–7).

The anion asymptote energies in Table3 (p.4) are rounded published atomic
constraints, not repository atomic predictions or a precise fine-structure-
resolved EA benchmark. Bound-state MRCI+Q is appropriate below the neutral
continuum; the paper distinguishes that region from resonant electronic states
(p.4). It does not certify that a restricted molecular CCSD(T) curve reaches the
correct free-fragment limit. These facts were checked in the local primary-paper
extraction with the declared cover-page offset; no numerical reproduction is
claimed.

## Terminology map

- cc40/cc50 → number of target states retained in the close-coupling expansion
  (p. 6, Table 4).
- CAS(10,n) → ten active electrons in n spatial orbitals, excluding the two
  frozen core orbitals (p. 3, Table 1).
- B1/B2 → degenerate components containing Pi symmetry in C2v (p. 6).

## Not used here

High-lying Feshbach resonance assignments, the full45-state asymptotic
enumeration, numerical MRCI+Q anion curves, and historical hardware timings.
