## Why this repository cares

This is the paper that extends ECS from the time-independent scattering theory covered by
`reference/literature/rescigno-2000-pra62-032706.md` and
`reference/literature/mccurdy-2004-jpb37-r137.md` into the **time-dependent** Schrodinger
equation — pairing ECS with a Crank-Nicolson-type propagator on complex-symmetric matrices. That
combination is the direct conceptual ancestor of `qscat.evolution.make_cn_stepper` /
`make_sparse_cn_stepper` / `make_pade_stepper`, used throughout the TD cross-section and TD-DA
work (`n2_td_cross_section`, `n2_2d_td_cross_section`, `qscat.core.time_dependent`,
`qscat.core.td_extractors`). Three things it establishes that the two TI-focused notes above do
not:

1. An explicit condition on the ECS rotation angle for a free/short-range time-dependent
   wavepacket to vanish beyond the pivot — `theta < pi/4` — derived for a Gaussian test packet.
   This is a *different* angle bound from both the general resonance-decay condition in the 2004
   review and the H2+ potential-analyticity bound in `docs/physics/lcp-resonance-levels.md` §6
   (`theta_nuclear < pi/8`, `theta_electronic < pi/4`) — the three conditions come from different
   physical requirements (free-packet boundedness, resonance-pole exposure, and potential-term
   analyticity respectively) and happen to share no common derivation.
2. The generalized Cayley/Crank-Nicolson propagator for complex-symmetric (non-Hermitian) `H`,
   `S` matrices under ECS — the historical prototype of the propagator qscat implements.
3. A genuine two-radial-coordinate (electron-hydrogen collision) time-dependent ECS demonstration
   (Fig. 7) — structurally the same class of problem as `n2_2d_td_cross_section`'s two-coordinate
   (electronic x nuclear) propagation, predating it by roughly a decade and a half, though for a
   different (three-body Coulomb) system.

## Equations

```
C(x) = x,                    x < x_M                                 p. 5982, Eq. (8)   [single-sided ECS contour]
     = (x-x_M) e^{i theta} + x_M,  x >= x_M
D(x) = (x+x_M) e^{i theta} - x_M,  x <= -x_M                          p. 5982, Eq. (8)   [double-sided ECS contour]
     = x,                          -x_M < x < x_M
     = (x-x_M) e^{i theta} + x_M,  x >= x_M
Psi(C(x),t) = exp[(i/hbar) alpha_t (C(x)-x_t)^2                       p. 5983, Eq. (14)  [free Gaussian on the contour]
              + (i/hbar) p_t (C(x)-x_t) + (i/hbar) gamma_t]
lim_{x->infinity} Psi(C(x)) = 0   for  0 < theta < pi/4                p. 5983, Eq. (19)  [boundedness condition]
c(t+dt) = U(dt) c(t)                                                   p. 5985, Eq. (23)  [Crank-Nicolson update]
U = [S + (i/2hbar) H dt]^{-1} [S + (i/2hbar) H dt]^*                   p. 5985, Eq. (24)  [real-coordinate CN propagator]
U~ = [S~ + (i/2hbar) H~ dt]^{-1} [S~ - (i/2hbar) H~ dt]                p. 5985, Eq. (32)  [complex-symmetric CN propagator under ECS]
```

Atomic units throughout (`hbar=1`, `m_e=1`, `e=1`, p. 5985) — the same convention
`libs/qscat/qscat/units.py` uses.

## Parameters and numeric values

No molecular or model constants are published — this is a methods paper using generic model
potentials (`V(x)=exp(-x^2/2)`, a dc/ac field Hamiltonian, and a simple e-H radial model). The
numeric grid/convergence parameters above (Fig. 4, Fig. 7) are the paper's own internal
demonstrations, not values checked against repo code.

## Findings and limits

- The `theta < pi/4` bound (Eq. 19-20) is derived specifically for a free (or short-range-potential,
  once beyond the interaction region) Gaussian wavepacket's quadratic phase remaining bounded on
  the rotated contour — it is not a general statement about resonance exposure or potential
  analyticity, and the paper does not claim it generalizes beyond this packet form.
- Sec. V (Conclusion) explicitly states two open limits: (1) the paper has "not established the
  entire class of Hamiltonians for which complex exterior scaling is applicable in the TDSE," and
  (2) it has "not even given a proof for the Coulomb potential — for which the procedure evidently
  works perfectly" — i.e., the Coulomb case is empirically demonstrated (Sec. IV D's charged
  two-electron example, not extracted here) but not formally justified.
- A "time's arrow" consistency condition is noted (p. 5986): forward propagation (`t->+infinity`)
  requires the scaling angle's sign to match the outgoing momentum's sign; backward propagation
  (`t->-infinity`) requires the *conjugated* contour. qscat only propagates forward in time, so
  this condition is not currently exercised, but would need attention if backward propagation
  were ever added.
- Larger scaling angles decay the packet faster past the pivot but need more elements near the
  pivot for accurate representation (p. 5987) — the same accuracy/cost tradeoff `qscat.tuning`'s
  ECS-tail sizing (`qscat.tuning.ecs`) balances, though not derived from this specific tradeoff
  statement.

## Not used here

- Sec. IV B-C (a charged particle in dc and ac electromagnetic fields — the Stark-effect and
  Volkov/Floquet-gauge treatments) — not a qscat use case; qscat has no external-field
  Hamiltonians.
- Sec. IV D's worked two-electron Coulomb (e-H radial collision) example (Fig. 7) beyond the
  structural precedent already noted — no formulas transcribed or reused; it is a three-body
  Coulomb system, not qscat's 2-electron/nuclear-coordinate diatomic model.
- The "time's arrow" backward-propagation discussion (p. 5986, conjugated-contour construction) —
  not needed since qscat only propagates forward in time.
- Sec. III's tent-function finite-element formalism (Eqs. 21-31) as a basis construction — qscat
  uses the FEM-DVR (Gauss-Lobatto) basis of Rescigno-McCurdy 2000 instead; only the propagator
  form built on top of it (Eq. 32) is a genuine precedent.
