# D. J. Tannor, D. E. Weeks, J. Chem. Phys. 98, 3884 (1993) — Wave packet correlation function formulation of scattering theory

**Source:** `reference/literature/tannor-1993-jcp98-3884.pdf` (gitignored) · DOI 10.1063/1.464016
**Pagination:** per-article, 3884 … 3893 (extractor page N = printed page 3883+N; offset zero,
checked at page 1 ["3884 J. Chem. Phys. 98 (5)..."] and page 10 ["...formulation 3893"]).
The extracted `.txt` has heavy OCR ligature corruption (a 1990s two-column AIP scan: "Mdller"
for "Møller", garbled subscripts/exponents in nearly every displayed equation). The equations
below were reconstructed from the legible surrounding text and cross-checked against the
standard Møller-operator/S-matrix formalism the paper itself cites (Taylor 1972, Newton 1966) —
flagged inline wherever a specific line could not be read with full confidence.

## Why this repository cares

This is the paper that defines "the Tannor-Weeks method": propagate two wave packets (one
representing the reactant/incident channel, one the product/outgoing channel) under the full
Hamiltonian, form their time-domain correlation function, and Fourier-transform it to obtain an
individual, energy-resolved S-matrix element — one propagation yielding S(E) at every energy at
once. `qscat.core.td_extractors.TannorWeeks` (and the two siblings `Dirac`/`Flux` built next to
it) is a direct implementation of this method for electron-diatomic vibrational-excitation (VE)
and dissociative-attachment (DA) cross sections. Without this source, the `eta_incident`/
`eta_outgoing` deconvolution factors in `qscat.core.correlation`, and the elastic-channel
free-reference subtraction the repo has been bitten by twice (`td-elastic-wavepacket-
normalization` memory), would be unmotivated numerical machinery rather than a documented
feature of the transform itself: the paper states explicitly that its outgoing normalization is
not 1 (below), which is exactly the fact qModeling rediscovered empirically before finding it
here.

## Equations

Atomic-unit convention note: the paper writes `ħ` explicitly throughout (SI-style), unlike
`libs/qscat/qscat/units.py`'s atomic units (`ħ=1`); the transcriptions below keep the paper's own
`ħ` for fidelity, and every `ħ` is silently 1 wherever the repo's transform is compared to it.

```
Omega_pm = lim_{t -> -+inf} e^{iHt/hbar} e^{-iH0^gamma t/hbar}        p. 3885, Eq. (2)
Omega_pm^dagger Omega_pm = 1  (isometry)                              p. 3885, Eq. (3)
Omega_pm^dagger = Omega_pm^{-1}  (no bound states)                    p. 3885, Eq. (4)
phi^pm = Omega_pm^dagger phi                                          p. 3885, Eq. (5)
S = Omega_-^dagger Omega_+                                            p. 3885, Eq. (8)
S_{beta,alpha} delta(E-E') = <psi_{beta,E'}| S |psi_{alpha,E}>        p. 3887, Eq. (25)
psi_{alpha,E} = Int_{-inf}^{inf} e^{-iHt/hbar + iEt/hbar} phi_alpha dt   p. 3887, Eq. (26)
phi_alpha^0(R,r) = chi_n(r) g_alpha(R)                                p. 3887, Eq. (27)
phi_alpha = Int eta_alpha(E) psi_{alpha,E} dE                         p. 3887, Eq. (29)
eta_alpha(E) = <psi_{alpha,E} | phi_alpha>                            p. 3887, Eq. (30)
eta_alpha(E) = (2 pi hbar)^{-1/2} Int e^{-ikR} g_alpha(R) dR,  k = sqrt(2mE)/hbar   p. 3887, Eq. (32)
S_{beta,alpha}(E) = (2 pi hbar)^{-1} / [eta_beta^*(E) eta_alpha(E)]
    * Int_{-inf}^{inf} <phi_beta^-| e^{-iHt/hbar} |phi_alpha^+> e^{iEt/hbar} dt      p. 3888, Eq. (39)
S_{beta',alpha'}(E) = S_{beta,alpha}(E)   (time reversal / detailed balance)         p. 3888-3889, Eq. (50)
```

The repo's own transform (`time_dependent.py` module docstring; `td_extractors.TannorWeeks`) is:

```
S_{v->v'}(E) = [2*pi*conj(eta_out_{v'}(E))*eta_in_v(E)]^{-1}
               * sum_n w_n exp(i*E_tot*t_n) c_{v'}(t_n) * dt
```

**Parity check (structural, run by hand against the transcription above):** the repo's `2*pi`
matches the paper's `2*pi*hbar` at `hbar=1`; the repo's `conj(eta_out)*eta_in` is the paper's
`eta_beta^*(E)*eta_alpha(E)` with `beta<->out`, `alpha<->in`; the repo's `sum_n w_n exp(...)
c_{v'}(t_n) dt` is a quadrature realization of the paper's `Int <phi_beta^-|e^{-iHt/hbar}|
phi_alpha^+> e^{iEt/hbar} dt` with `c_{v'}(t_n) = c_product(Phi_{v'}, psi(t_n))` standing in for
the bra-ket correlation function. The two formulas are the same object; the repo generalizes
`g_alpha(R)`'s plain plane-wave Fourier transform (Eq. 32, a 1-D Cartesian free particle) to
`eta_incident`/`eta_outgoing`'s overlap with an energy-normalized partial-wave radial function
(`riccati_bessel_en`/`riccati_hankel_en`, spherical rather than Cartesian asymptotics) — the same
generalization Eq. (27)'s `chi_n(r) g_alpha(R)` already anticipates (an internal/channel quantum
number times a translational packet), which is structurally identical to `correlation.
outgoing_channel`'s `Phi_v' = g_out(r) chi_v'(R)`.

## Findings and limits

- **The transform is only trustworthy inside the initial wave packet's own energy width.** In
  the paper's 1-D barrier demonstration, the calculated transmission coefficient "begins to
  deviate significantly from the analytic result" once the energy moves away from where the
  initial Gaussian has appreciable spectral density; extending the usable range requires
  re-centering or broadening the packet in energy — p. 3891. This is the same failure mode
  `docs/physics/n2-2d-td-cross-section.md` documents as the "usable spectral window" (checked
  against the repo: `(0.06, 0.21)` Ha on the N₂ working grid, computed by
  `convergence.usable_window(frac=0.5)` from where `|eta_incident(E)|` falls below half its
  peak) — not a repo-specific artifact, but the method's own documented limit.
- **Rapid correlation-function decay is a simplifying case, not a requirement.** The paper
  expects the correlation function to "die off rapidly, at least for direct reactions" (p. 3889),
  permitting a compact grid and short propagation — but explicitly notes the opposite case:
  "when there are resonances, the correlation function should persist over an extended period of
  time, leading to structure in `S_βα` as a function of `E`" (p. 3892). qModeling's N₂ shape
  resonance is exactly this second case (`||Psi(t)||` decays from `1.0` to `0.024` only by
  `T=1500`, not quickly), and the repo's own finite-`T` convergence checks
  (`test_v4_finite_t_stability_and_depletion`) exist because of it, not despite it.
  A full column of the S matrix (many `v'`, or all three extractor methods) is obtained from one
  shared propagation by tracking a whole set of `{φ_β}` channel functions in parallel (p. 3892,
  Conclusions) — the same design `td_ve_cross_sections_all`/`propagate(..., extractors=[...])`
  implements.
- **Detailed balance is a structural property of Eq. (39), not something the paper needs a
  numerical example to prove.** `S_β'α'(E) = S_βα(E)` follows from the symmetric (Ω₋ on one
  side, Ω₊ on the other) form of the correlation function itself — Sec. IV derives it formally,
  not from the barrier-scattering example.
- The paper's own scope: a formal method paper plus a single 1-D two-state (transmission/
  reflection) proof-of-concept; it makes no claim about multichannel, resonant, or
  many-degree-of-freedom systems beyond the general expectation quoted above. Every
  multichannel/DVR/ECS/resonance-specific detail in `qscat.core` is the repo's own extension of
  the method, not something this paper validates.

## Terminology map

| Paper symbol | qModeling name |
|---|---|
| `φ_α` (incoming/reactant wave packet) | the incident Gaussian, `wp_in` (`qscat.core.wavepacket.gaussian_coeffs`) |
| `φ_β` (outgoing/product wave packet) | the outgoing test packet, `wp_out`; `Phi_v' = correlation.outgoing_channel(...)` |
| `χ_n(r) g_α(R)` (Eq. 27, internal state × translational packet) | `Phi_v' = g_out(r) chi_v'(R)` (electronic axis) / `Phi_c = phi_c(r) g_out(R)` (nuclear axis, `outgoing_channel_nuclear`) |
| `η_α(E)`, `η_β(E)` | `eta_incident`, `eta_outgoing` (`qscat.core.correlation`) |
| `C_βα(t) = <φ_β^-\|e^{-iHt/ħ}\|φ_α^+>` | `c_{v'}(t)` — `TannorWeeks.record`'s per-step `c_product(Phi_{v'}, psi)` |
| `S_βα(E)` (Eq. 39) | `S[j]` in `_s_vector_one_energy`/`TannorWeeks.sigma` (or the `_tw_da_*`/`_dirac_*`/`_flux_*` siblings for the `Dirac`/`Flux` extractors, which replace `η_β(E)`/`C_βα(t)` with a point value or a Wronskian pair but keep the same `S_βα(E)` role) |
| Møller operators `Ω_±` | not represented as explicit operators anywhere in the code — `qscat` realizes their effect numerically by propagating `psi(t)` under the full `H` and projecting onto channel functions, rather than by constructing `Ω_±` as objects |
| `T` (time-reversal operator, Sec. IV) | not implemented |

## Not used here

- The reactive-scattering (multi-arrangement-channel, A+BC/AB+C Jacobi-coordinate) framing of
  Secs. I-II — qscat's problem has a single arrangement channel per axis (electronic VE, or a
  distinct nuclear DA rearrangement channel), so only the single-channel machinery (Eqs. 26-39)
  is used; the paper's discussion of switching interaction representations/coordinate systems
  between reactant and product legs does not apply.
- Sec. IV's formal proof of time-reversal symmetry (Eqs. 50-63) — not directly tested by the
  repo. (The repo's own "ratios bracket 1.0" genuineness check in
  `docs/physics/n2-2d-td-cross-section.md` is an unrelated diagnostic against a differential
  oracle, not a test of Eq. 50.)
- Sec. V's discussion of semiclassical and interaction-representation (IR) propagation for the
  reactant/product legs (refs. 5-14) — qscat always propagates the full quantum wavefunction on
  the grid (a Padé-stepped Crank-Nicolson-family propagator) for every leg; no semiclassical
  trajectories or IR-based propagation are used anywhere.
- Sec. VI's 1-D barrier-scattering numerical demonstration (Figs. 2-6) — read only for its
  qualitative finding about the energy-width limitation (used above); none of its Gaussian/
  barrier parameters, transmission/reflection data, or figures are transcribed or checked
  against the repo.
- Eq. (1) and the general arrangement-channel Hamiltonian splitting `H = H_0^γ + V^γ` — qscat's
  Hamiltonians are built directly per `qscat.model`/`qscat.dvr`, not through this paper's
  formalism.
