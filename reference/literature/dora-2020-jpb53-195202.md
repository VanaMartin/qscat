**Source:** `reference/literature/dora-2020-jpb53-195202.pdf` (gitignored) ·

**Pagination:** article printed pp. 1–9, with an unnumbered publisher cover;
extractor page = printed page + 1.

## Why this repository cares

This larger CO calculation supplies an important counterexample to equating
a larger atomic basis with a converged low-energy resonance. It also documents
geometry-dependent orbital/target-state tracking problems and fit-sensitive
widths that matter for a fixed-nuclei campaign intended to constrain a smooth
two-dimensional potential.

## Findings and limits

The large basis was chosen to represent higher-lying resonances (p. 3).
The resulting lowest shape-resonance width differs substantially from the
2016 model (p. 4, Table 2); this is not evidence that either model has a known
small error bar. Target-state tracking problems restrict geometry coverage
(pp. 2–3), and widths remain fit-sensitive (pp. 5–6). Good electronic-excitation
agreement is reported for the energy region of interest to the higher-lying
study (p. 4), rather than a convergence certificate for low-energy nuclear
dynamics.

## Terminology map

- 27 states → 41 symmetry components when Pi and Delta degeneracies are
  counted separately (p. 3).
- R → `bond_length`, the internuclear coordinate, distinct from the sphere
  radius a (p. 3).
- Er / Gamma → resonance position / full width relative to the neutral
  threshold (p. 4, Table 2).

The eight higher-lying resonance curves and their parent-state assignments,
