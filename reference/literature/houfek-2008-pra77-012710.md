## Why this repository cares

This is "paper II", the direct sequel to Houfek/Rescigno/McCurdy 2006 (PRA 73, 032721 —
`houfek-2006-pra73-032721.md`, "paper I"), reusing paper I's exact 2-D model but adding
the **nonlocal resonance theory** (Feshbach projection-operator formalism) as a second,
more elaborate approximation to compare against the exact solution alongside the plain
local-complex-potential (LCP) reduction paper I already introduced. `qscat.core.nrm`
**implements that nonlocal model** — Sec. IV's ECS+DVR discretization scheme, Eq. (55)-(61),
is the specification this package is written against, Eq. (52)/(54) its nuclear
equation and dissociative-attachment cross section, and Eq. (28)-(38) the
resonant/background T-matrix decomposition its vibrational-excitation route is written
against. Beyond the code, the repo takes the
paper's central methodological finding: **the outcome of any resonance approximation
depends on how the "discrete state" φ_d is chosen**, and the paper enumerates and tests
several choices, including the one qscat's LCP construction (`qscat.ecs.find_resonance_pole`,
`qscat.core.lcp.local_complex_potential`) already uses implicitly. Without this source,
the repo's documented LCP failure modes — missing non-resonant elastic background,
divergent near-threshold behavior (`docs/physics/diatomic-ve-cross-sections.md`) — would
be observed facts with no explanation; this paper supplies the explanation (omitted
background T-matrix terms and an energy-independent width) and independently predicts
both, in the same F₂ model this repo runs.

### The resonant/background T-matrix decomposition, Eq. (28)-(38)

Sec. III B, p. 012710-3–4. This is the specification the NRM's vibrational-excitation
route is written against. `J^l_k(r)` is the unperturbed free radial wave. `phi_k^+` are the
energy-normalized background scattering states of Eq. (18), whose boundary condition "is
determined by the incoming wave `J^l_k(r)`"; `phi_k^-` are defined "in a similar way … but
with a different boundary condition in which `J^l_k(r)` stands for an outgoing wave function"
(p. 012710-3, both quoted). The `±` labels are transcribed as the paper attaches them, to the
boundary condition on `J^l_k`; `phi_k^-` is the state Eq. (31) and Eq. (37) project onto.

```
H  = H0 + V1 + V2                                                 p. 012710-3, Eq. (24)
V1 = P Hel P - Tr - l(l+1)/(2 r^2) = P Hel P - Hel + Vint         p. 012710-3, Eq. (25)
V2 = Hel - P Hel P                                                p. 012710-3, Eq. (26)
H0 + V1 = TR + V0 + P Hel P                                       p. 012710-3, Eq. (27)

T^VE_{vi->vf} = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| V1 |chi_vi J^l_ki>
              + <chi_vf phi_kf^-| V2 |Psi^+>                      p. 012710-3, Eq. (28)

|Psi^+> = |chi_vi phi_ki^+>
          + (E - H + i eta)^{-1} V2 |chi_vi phi_ki^+>             p. 012710-3, Eq. (29)

T^res_{vi->vf} = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| Hel - P Hel P |Psi^+>
               = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| P Hel Q |Psi^+>                p. 012710-4, Eq. (30)

T^res_{vi->vf} = <chi_vf| Vdkf^-* |Psi_d^+>,
    with Psi_d^+(R) = <phi_d|Psi^+>_r  (integration over r only)  p. 012710-4, Eq. (31)

<phi_kf^-|phi_k^+>          != delta(kf^2/2 - k^2/2)              p. 012710-4, Eq. (32)
<phi_kf^-| P Hel Q |phi_d>  != <phi_kf^+| Hel |phi_d>             p. 012710-4, Eq. (33)

phi_k^-(r) = (phi_k^+(r))*        [radial case, REAL discrete state]
                                                                  p. 012710-4, Eq. (34)
<phi_kf^-| P Hel Q |phi_d> = <phi_kf^-|Hel|phi_d> = <phi_d|Hel|phi_kf^+>
                           = Vdkf^+     [same special case; Hel Hermitian]
                                                                  p. 012710-4, Eq. (35)
phi_kvec^- = (phi_{-kvec}^+)*     [three-dimensional case]        p. 012710-4, Eq. (36)

T^bg_{vi->vf} = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| P Hel P - Hel + Vint |chi_vi J^l_ki>
              = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| Vint - P Hel Q |chi_vi J^l_ki>
              = <chi_vf phi_kf^-| Vint |chi_vi J^l_ki>
                - <chi_vf| Vdkf^-* J^l_{d ki} |chi_vi>            p. 012710-4, Eq. (37)

J^l_{d ki}(R) = Int dr phi_d*(r;R) J^l_ki(r)                      p. 012710-4, Eq. (38)
```

Conditions the paper attaches to these:

- **Eq. (28) is the two-potential formula, not a stated `T = T_bg + T_res` identity.**
  The paper obtains Eq. (28) from the two-potential formula (citing Ref. [16], p. 202)
  and only then identifies its terms in prose: the second term "corresponds to the
  resonant part of the T matrix" (p. 012710-4), the first "is generally called the
  background scattering T matrix" (p. 012710-4). Eq. (30)/(31) develop the former,
  Eq. (37) the latter, so `T^VE = T^bg + T^res` is a consequence of Eq. (28) rather
  than an equation the paper numbers.
- **Which coupling appears in Eq. (31), and when.** The general form carries
  `Vdkf^-*` — the complex conjugate of `Vdk^-`, the coupling to `phi_k^-` defined in
  Eq. (23), NOT of `Vdk^+` of Eq. (21). The paper states plainly that this "differs slightly from
  the result of Domcke [Ref. [6], Eq. (4.14)] where the matrix `Vdk` without a
  superscript, which corresponds to the matrix element `Vdk^+` defined by Eq. (21),
  was, in our opinion, used incorrectly" (p. 012710-4, quoted), justifying it by
  Eq. (32)-(33). **Eq. (34) is the condition that selects the implementable form:** for
  the radial case with a real discrete state, `phi_k^- = (phi_k^+)*`, and Eq. (35)
  collapses the matrix element to `Vdkf^+` — the paper's own emphasis, "in this special
  case we can use the matrix element `Vdk^+` but *without complex conjugation*"
  (p. 012710-4). Eq. (36) is the three-dimensional replacement, `phi_kvec^- =
  (phi_{-kvec}^+)*`, under which "`V*_{d kvec}` in Eq. (4.14) of [6] should be replaced
  by `V_{d,-kvec}` under the assumption that `phi_d` is real, otherwise `V^-*_{d kvec}`
  must be used" (p. 012710-4, quoted). qscat's model is radial with a single fixed `l`
  and a real `phi_d`, so Eq. (34)/(35) is the branch that applies, and the contraction
  to implement is the non-conjugated `Vdk^+` one. That is bilinear, consistent with —
  but argued independently of — the no-conjugation scalar product the paper separately
  requires of the ECS states on p. 012710-6 (`qscat.linalg.c_product`): Eq. (35) drops
  the conjugation by Hermiticity of `Hel` plus Eq. (34), while p. 012710-6 drops it
  because `P Hel P` is complex symmetric under exterior complex scaling. Two arguments,
  the same non-conjugated contraction. The general `Vdkf^-*` form of Eq. (31) and the 3-D
  `phi_{-kvec}^+` form of Eq. (36) are recorded here so that the branch taken is a
  documented choice with a stated condition, not an implicit one.
- **Why the difference is not cosmetic.** The paper notes the discrepancy "becomes
  important when the background terms defined below are added to the resonant T matrix,
  since the coupling matrix elements `Vdk^±` are in general complex even when the
  discrete state is real" (p. 012710-4). Eq. (37) carries the same `Vdkf^-*`, so the
  two terms must be built from the same convention or their sum is wrong even where
  each is individually small.
- **`phi_d` is conjugated in Eq. (38)** as printed, `Int dr phi_d*(r;R) J^l_ki(r)`,
  which is a no-op for the real discrete state Eq. (34)/(35) already assumes.
- **The background terms are not negligible.** They "are nonzero even for inelastic
  vibrational excitation, but generally small when compared to the resonant part of
  the T matrix", with the F₂-like model of Sec. VI the counterexample (p. 012710-4).
- **`Vdk^±` here are at specific real electron energies** and must be built from
  Eq. (21)/(23) directly, not from the discretized `V_dn` of Eq. (59) — see the
  Eq. (55)-(61) conditions below.

### The ECS + DVR evaluation scheme, Eq. (55)-(61)

Sec. IV, p. 012710-5–6. This is the specification `qscat.core.nrm` implements. `w_i` are
the **electronic** DVR quadrature weights, `W_i` the **nuclear** ones; `[R_j]` are the
nuclear DVR nodes and `[r_i]` the electronic ones, both exterior-complex-scaled.

```
F(E,R,R') = <phi_d| H_el P [P(E - H_el - T_R - V_0 + i eta)P]^{-1} P H_el |phi_d>_r
                                                                  p. 012710-5, Eq. (55)
sum_k (P H_el P)_{i,k} phi_n(r_k;R_j) = E_n(R_j) phi_n(r_i;R_j)   p. 012710-6, Eq. (56)
P(R_j)_{i,k} = delta_{i,k} - Q(R_j)_{i,k}                          p. 012710-6, Eq. (57)
Q(R_j)_{i,k} = sqrt(w_i) phi_d(r_i;R_j) phi_d(r_k;R_j) sqrt(w_k)   p. 012710-6, Eq. (58)
V_dn(R_j) = Int dr phi_d(r;R_j) H_el(r,R_j) phi_n(r;R_j)
          = Int dr phi_n(r;R_j) H_el(r,R_j) phi_d(r;R_j)
          ~= sum_{i,k} sqrt(w_i) phi_d(r_i;R_j) H_el(R_j)_{i,k}
                                 phi_n(r_k;R_j) sqrt(w_k)          p. 012710-6, Eq. (59)
F(E,R_i,R_j) = sum_n sqrt(W_i) V_dn(R_i) M(n)^{-1}_{i,j}
                               V_dn(R_j) sqrt(W_j)                 p. 012710-6, Eq. (60)
M(n)_{i,j} = [E - T_R - V_0(R) - E_n(R)]_{i,j}                     p. 012710-6, Eq. (61)
```

Conditions the paper attaches to these, each load-bearing for the implementation:

- **No conjugation.** `P H_el P` under complex scaling "is symmetric, but not Hermitian,
  [so] we have to use for the wave functions `phi_n(r;R)` the scalar product defined
  without complex conjugation" (p. 012710-6). Eq. (59) and Eq. (60) are therefore written
  bilinearly — note that Eq. (60) carries `V_dn(R_j)`, *not* `V_dn(R_j)*`, even though the
  undiscretized Eq. (53) it evaluates is written with `Vdk^+(R')*`. The bilinear form is
  the one to implement under ECS.
- **`phi_d` real and inner.** Eq. (59) holds "assum[ing] that the discrete state is real
  and localized in the inner region where the electronic coordinate is not complex scaled"
  (p. 012710-6). That is why Eq. (59)'s two integrals are stated as equal.
- **`E_n` is R-dependent.** Eq. (56) is solved "for each discrete value of the nuclear
  coordinate `R_j`" (p. 012710-5–6), and Eq. (61) carries `E_n(R)` inside the nuclear
  matrix, so `M(n)` is `E - T_R` minus the **R-varying** diagonal `V_0(R) + E_n(R)`.
- **No regulator needed.** "We have avoided singularities in constructing the inverse of
  `M(n)` since the energies `E_n(R)` are complex whereas the total energy `E` of the
  system is always real" (p. 012710-6) — the `i eta` of Eq. (55) is not carried into the
  discretized form.
- **`Vdk+` is not `V_dn`.** The couplings on Eq. (52)'s right-hand side and in the T
  matrices of Eq. (31)/(37) "are at specific *real* electron energies and hence must be
  evaluated directly using Eq. (21)", with `phi_k^+` from Eq. (18) solved in the
  electronic DVR basis under ECS (p. 012710-6). The discretized `V_dn` of Eq. (59) serve
  only to build `F`.
- **Weight convention.** Eq. (58) and Eq. (59) fix the paper's convention: `sqrt(weight) x
  function value = DVR coefficient`, and operator matrices such as `(P H_el P)_{i,k}` and
  `H_el(R_j)_{i,k}` are coefficient-space. Read that way, the nuclear-coefficient-space
  matrix of `F` is `sum_n V_dn(R_i) M(n)^{-1}_{i,j} V_dn(R_j)` with **no** surviving
  `sqrt(W)` factors, since the two `sqrt(W_i)`, `sqrt(W_j)` that convert `Psi_d` to and
  from coefficients are exactly the ones already inside `M(n)^{-1}`. Eq. (60)'s printed
  `sqrt(W_i)`/`sqrt(W_j)` are consistent only if its `M(n)^{-1}_{i,j}` is read as the
  function-space Green's-function *kernel* value rather than the inverse of the
  coefficient-space matrix Eq. (61) defines. The paper does not resolve the ambiguity;
  `qscat.core.nrm.nonlocal_potential` works in coefficient space and applies no `sqrt(W)`,
  which is the reading whose local limit reduces correctly to `diag(-(i/2) Gamma(E,R))`.

## Findings and limits

- **The central methodological point (why the repo cites this paper):** the "discrete
  state" `φ_d(r;R)` is not unique, and the nonlocal-model results — and, this note argues,
  the qualitative behavior of any resonance approximation built on a fixed-R electronic
  quantity — depend materially on which choice is made. Three choices are tested:
  - **(A) "Physical"/intuitive** — `φ_d` built from the fixed-R resonant electronic
    scattering wavefunction (real part of the ECS pole energy), truncated smoothly —
    p. 012710-7–9, Sec. VI A. This is the quantity qscat's `find_resonance_pole` computes
    and `local_complex_potential` uses directly as `Vd(R)`/`Gamma(R)`; the paper states
    it "almost coincides with" `Eres(R)` (p. 012710-8). Result: **can break down**
    (Born-Oppenheimer failure) at small R where `φ_d` varies rapidly with R, most
    severely for DA (no background term available to compensate) and for NO/F₂ (larger,
    more R-sensitive resonance width than N₂) — p. 012710-8–9.
  - **(B) R-independent** — `φ_d = φ_b` (the R→∞ bound state) for all R — p. 012710-9–10,
    Sec. VI B. Result: **essentially exact** for VE in all three molecules, provided the
    background T-matrix terms (Eq. 37 of this paper) are included; without them, elastic
    and even some inelastic VE channels are wrong. The paper states the same of DA, but
    demonstrates it for **F₂ alone** — see the silence on N₂/NO DA below.
  - **(C) "Compact"** — a smoothly-R-varying, more localized `φ_d` tuned to minimize
    background contributions — p. 012710-10–11, Sec. VI C. Result: for F₂ (the molecule
    with the largest resonance width and thus largest background terms), certain tunings
    reproduce the exact resonant-only T-matrix (Eq. 31) well even **without** background
    terms, though the paper stresses this is model-specific, not a general recipe.
- **What this implies for qscat's plain LCP** (not the nonlocal model at all — qscat
  builds only `V_res(R) = E_res(R) - iΓ(R)/2`, paper I's/this paper's short-dashed
  curve): the paper's own comparisons show this bare curve, with **no** discrete-state
  refinement and **no** background T-matrix, systematically (i) gets DA threshold
  behavior wrong because its width is energy-independent (`Γ(R)`, not `Γ(E,R)` of
  Eq. 68) — p. 012710-9; and (ii) omits the non-resonant background contribution to
  elastic and some inelastic VE cross sections, which this paper shows is *not*
  negligible, especially for F₂ — p. 012710-1 (abstract), 012710-9–10. Both are exactly
  the qualitative departures `docs/physics/diatomic-ve-cross-sections.md` documents for
  qscat's own F₂/NO LCP runs (near-threshold σ_DA under-prediction, missing elastic
  background) — an independent, published confirmation of the failure mode, not merely
  a coincidence of this repo's implementation.
- **The paper is SILENT on N₂ and NO dissociative attachment.** The sentence "For
  dissociative attachment, where there are no background contributions, the nonlocal model
  with an R-independent choice of discrete state gives exact results" (p. 012710-10) is
  supported by **one** published comparison, in **one** molecule. The panel inventory,
  read off the figures:

  | Figure | Panels |
  |---|---|
  | Fig. 4, p. 012710-8 (N₂, physical `φ_d`) | `N2 VE 0->0`, `N2 VE 0->1`, `N2 VE 0->8` |
  | Fig. 5, p. 012710-9 (NO, physical `φ_d`) | `NO VE 0->1`, `NO VE 0->2` |
  | Fig. 6, p. 012710-9 (F₂, physical `φ_d`) | `F2 VE 0->1`, **`F2 DA`** |
  | Fig. 8, p. 012710-10 (all three, R-independent `φ_d`) | `N2 VE 0->0`, `N2 VE 0->8`, `NO VE 0->1`, `NO VE 0->2`, `F2 VE 0->1`, **`F2 DA (v_i=0)`** |

  **No N₂ or NO dissociative-attachment cross section appears anywhere in this paper**, for
  any discrete-state choice. Results for those two molecules are therefore an **extension
  beyond the paper's tested range**, not a comparison against it, and disagreement there
  cannot be read as contradicting the source.
- **What the Fig. 8 caption's "practically the same" does and does not assert.** The
  caption (p. 012710-10, quoted in full in the uses table) gives the reason the N₂ 0→1
  panel is dropped from Fig. 8. Two limits on how far it can be pushed. (i) **Which
  calculations.** Fig. 8's own curves are the four Fig. 4 defines — exact 2-D, LCP,
  nonlocal without background, nonlocal with background (p. 012710-8, Fig. 4 caption) — so
  "all calculations" includes the bare LCP, not only the nonlocal ones. (ii) **On what
  scale.** It is a statement about curves drawn on Fig. 4's LINEAR axis, which for the N₂
  0→1 panel runs to 14 `a0^2`; it is not a claim of a bounded *ratio*. Where the cross
  section is small the two are compatible: a route sitting at ~0.4× of a 0.3 `a0^2` value
  is ~1% of that axis and is invisible in print. This repo's own measurement
  (`validation/diatomic/test_ve_nrm.py`, 2026-08-18) is exactly that case — the LCP tracks
  exact to 5-8% across 0.07-0.12 hartrees where σ peaks near 10 `a0^2`, and falls to 0.379
  of exact only at E = 0.06 where σ_exact is 0.287 `a0^2`. The mechanism is the one this
  paper names at p. 012710-9: the LCP's resonance width is energy independent, so it
  degrades where the channel energy is far from `Eres`. **N₂ 0→1 is therefore not a
  counter-example to the caption.** Contrast the N₂ *elastic* panel, where the same repo
  run finds the LCP drifting 4.57 → 0.106 of exact *at large* σ — that one is a visible
  failure, and it is this paper's own missing-background claim (p. 012710-1, 012710-10).
- **Why only F₂ has a DA panel: the other two channels are energetically closed.** "The
  asymptote of the molecular-anion potential energy curve lies deeply below the ground
  vibrational state of the neutral molecule (as in the real electron-F₂ system) and
  therefore … there is no threshold in the dissociative attachment" (p. 012710-7). Fig. 7's
  left panels (p. 012710-9) show the asymptotes for all three models: `Vd(R->5 a0)` is
  roughly `-0.30` (N₂, still rising), `-0.065` (NO) and `-0.135` hartree (F₂), against
  `V0(R->inf) = 0`. Computed from `qscat.model` on this repo's own grids
  (`electronic_grid(r_max=24, order=8, n_complex=6)`, `nuclear_grid()`; 2026-08-17,
  reproduced independently), the DA channel threshold `Vd(inf) - eps_{v=0}` against the
  windows this paper plots:

  | | DA threshold | plotted windows (Figs. 4-6, 8) |
  |---|---|---|
  | N₂ | **+0.5016 Ha** | 0.05-0.17, 0.08-0.20 Ha |
  | NO | **+0.1719 Ha** | 0.01-0.08 Ha |
  | F₂ | **-0.0691 Ha** (open at any `E > 0`) | 0.00-0.10 Ha |

  NO's channel opens at more than **twice** the top of every NO window plotted; N₂'s at
  ~2.5×. Both are shut throughout everything PRA 77 published, which is why neither could
  have been tested. F₂'s negative threshold is the quoted "no threshold in the dissociative
  attachment", and is why its DA panel starts at `E = 0`.
- **A hypothesis, NOT a measured result, about what that implies near NO's threshold.**
  Just above `+0.1719 Ha` the DA exit momentum `K_DA = sqrt(2 mu (E - eps_e))` is small, so
  the nuclear wave crosses the region of large `Γ` slowly (Fig. 7's NO width panel reaches
  ~0.1 Ha) and the autodetachment survival factor `~exp(-Int Γ dR / v)` sits in an exponent
  of order tens. If so, agreement of the ingredients `Vd`, `Γ`, `E_n` at the 0.1-2.6% level
  — tight for a potential, loose for something appearing in an exponent — would place
  essentially no constraint on `σ_DA` there, and method-to-method differences of many
  orders of magnitude would be expected rather than diagnostic. F₂'s exit wave is fast
  (`K_DA ~ 58` on this repo's decks) and never passes through that gate. **The exponent has
  not been computed**; this is stated as a hypothesis consistent with the verified facts
  above, and nothing in the paper addresses it.
- **DA has no background-term analog** in the nonlocal formalism (Sec. III C, p. 012710-6):
  there is nothing to add back, so a DA cross section tests the discrete-state choice
  unbuffered, whereas VE (especially elastic) can be repaired by the background terms. Note
  the paper's two DA statements cut in opposite directions and are both F₂-only: choice A
  shows a visible Born-Oppenheimer breakdown in DA (p. 012710-9, Fig. 6), choice B does not
  (p. 012710-10, Fig. 8).
- The paper's own stated limits: it does not test real ab initio discrete-state
  definitions (stabilization methods, Feshbach-Fano R-matrix), only three well-defined
  parametrized choices within its own 2-D model; and it flags that hydrogen-containing
  diatomics (much smaller reduced mass than the ~1.3–1.7×10⁴ m_e systems studied here)
  would need further non-Born-Oppenheimer testing — p. 012710-12–13, Conclusion.

## Not used here

- The **three-dimensional** conjugation relation Eq. (36), `phi_kvec^- =
  (phi_{-kvec}^+)*`, and the `V_{d,-kvec}` replacement it implies for Domcke's Eq. (4.14).
  qscat's model is radial with a single fixed `l` and a real discrete state, so the
  branch it takes is Eq. (34)/(35); Eq. (36) is transcribed above only to record which
  condition selects which form. (The resonant/background decomposition itself, Eq. 28-38,
  IS used — see the Equations section.)
- The **"compact" discrete state of Sec. VI C** (Eq. 70-72, `λ_q(R)`, `λ_spec(R)`) — the
  third of the paper's three choices. `qscat.core.nrm.discrete_state` implements choices A
  (`PhysicalDiscreteState`, Sec. VI A) and B (`AsymptoticDiscreteState`, Sec. VI B) only;
  Sec. VI C's `λ_d(R)` are model-specific tuning functions used to demonstrate sensitivity,
  not physical constants worth porting.
- The **energy-normalized continuum convention** of Eq. (3)-(4), which differs from paper
  I's; the repo follows paper I's normalization (see that note).
- The Green's-function-expansion route to `F` that the paper rejects — expanding Eq. (51)
  in the molecular vibrational states `chi_v(R)`, which "leads to a singular integral over
  electron energies" requiring an assumed energy dependence of `Vdk+` (p. 012710-5). The
  ECS+DVR scheme of Eq. (55)-(61), which qscat implements, exists precisely to avoid it.
- Figs. 1-10 (potential surfaces, discrete-state wavefunction plots, cross-section
  comparison curves) are read for their qualitative conclusions only; no pixel/curve
  data was extracted.
- The Appendix's derivation that DA has no background term (Eq. A1-A7) is read for its
  conclusion only (recorded in Findings above), not transcribed as a derivation.
- N₂ and NO Table I entries here are unchanged from paper I and already covered by
  `houfek-2006-pra73-032721.md`; not repeated as a separate parity check.
