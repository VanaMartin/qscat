# M. Formánek, M. Váňa, K. Houfek, AIP Conf. Proc. 1281, 667 (2010) — Comparison of the Chebyshev Method and the Generalized Crank-Nicholson Method for Time Propagation in Quantum Mechanics

**Source:** `reference/literature/formanek-2010-aip1281-667.pdf` (gitignored) · DOI 10.1063/1.3498565
**Pagination:** printed 667–670; extractor page N = printed page 666+N (offset zero, checked at page 1 and page 4).

The confirmed title/authors differ from this note's original assignment: the paper is
a **method-efficiency comparison** (Chebyshev vs. the generalized Crank–Nicolson/Padé
propagator on a 1-D free-particle benchmark), not a paper on coupled radial differential
equations in multichannel scattering. It has no connection to
`docs/physics/angular-coupled-channels.md`'s parked coupled-channel direction or to
`libs/qscat/qscat/linalg/` — checked directly (no coupled-channel content anywhere in the
paper) and noted here so a later reader does not go looking for one.

## Why this repository cares

`qscat.evolution.make_pade_stepper`'s order-N diagonal-Padé propagator is exactly the
"generalized Crank-Nicholson method" this paper tests: its Eqs. (7)-(9) give the same
product-of-commuting-complex-root-factors form as `pade.py`'s docstring, and its
co-authors are Karel Houfek and **Martin Váňa — this repository's owner** (this is the
owner's own prior publication). The paper is background/motivating context, not the
equation-numbering source: `reference/literature/vandijk-2007-pre75-036707.md` (van Dijk
& Toyama 2007) is that source, already noted, and is the paper this one's Sec.
"Generalized Crank-Nicholson method" cites as origin (its ref. [3]). What this paper adds
is the empirical case for *choosing* the Padé/CN family over the Chebyshev alternative
under repeated-evaluation workloads — directly relevant because `reference/eMoScat`
(read via `port-scout`) offers **both** propagators as configurable options (`grep -l
Chebyshev reference/eMoScat/input/*/*.txt` finds it in NO's and other decks'
`approximation:` setting), while qModeling's `qscat.evolution` implements only the
Padé/CN family (`make_cn_stepper`, `make_sparse_cn_stepper`, `make_pade_stepper`) — no
Chebyshev stepper exists in `qscat`. This paper's own conclusion (below) is consistent
with, though not cited as the reason for, that choice: the repo's TD cross-section work
needs many wavefunction evaluations over a long propagation (correlation-function
sampling at every step, `docs/physics/n2-2d-td-cross-section.md`), the regime where this
paper finds the generalized Crank-Nicholson method becomes competitive with or faster
than Chebyshev.

## Equations

```
Psi(x, t+dt) = exp(-i H dt) Psi(x,t)                                          p. 667, Eq. (2)
exp(z) ~= [1 + a1 z + a2 z^2 + ... + aN z^N] / [1 - a1 z + a2 z^2 - ... + (-1)^N aN z^N]
        = prod_{s=1}^N (1 - z/z_s^(N)) / (1 + z/[z_s^(N)]*)                    p. 668, Eq. (7)  [diagonal Pade[N/N]]
Psi(x, t+dt) ~= prod_{s=1}^N K_s^(N) Psi(x,t)                                  p. 668, Eq. (8)
K_s^(N) = [1 + i H dt / z_s^(N)] / [1 - i H dt / (z_s^(N))*]                   p. 668, Eq. (9)  [evaluated at z = -iHdt]
H = -(1/2 mu) d^2/dx^2                                                        p. 668, Eq. (10)  [1-D free-particle test Hamiltonian]
```

Atomic units throughout (`ħ = m_e = 1`, p. 667) — matches `libs/qscat/qscat/units.py`; no
conversion needed.

## Parameters and numeric values

The paper's Table 1 (p. 668) lists the test problem's own numerical parameters (mass
μ=1836, initial packet x₀=10, p₀=-1, σ=1/√2, grid `x∈[-30,30]`, Δx=0.02, finite-difference
order r=8) and Table 2 (p. 669) the Δt/error/CPU-time sweep — these describe the paper's
own 1-D free-particle benchmark problem, not a molecular model or constant this repo
reuses; not applicable to compare against repo code.

## Findings and limits

- The paper's own conclusion (p. 669, "Conclusions"): which method is more efficient
  depends on the time step required by the physics — Chebyshev wins for small Δt (few
  terms needed), generalized CN wins for large Δt (few matrix inversions needed) — and the
  authors note the crossover also depends on computer architecture and implementation
  quality of the matrix multiply/inversion, not on the algorithms alone.
- The comparison is run on a single, simple 1-D free-particle system with an analytic
  reference solution (Eqs. 11-14, from Tannor's textbook, ref. [9]) — the paper does not
  test either method on a resonant, absorbing (ECS), or multi-dimensional Hamiltonian, so
  its efficiency numbers do not transfer quantitatively to the repo's N₂/NO/F₂ TD
  cross-section problems.
- This is a 4-page conference proceedings paper (ICNAAM 2010), not a peer-reviewed
  journal article with the depth of van Dijk & Toyama (2007); it is read here as
  supporting/motivating context for the propagator family choice, not as a numerical
  method specification.

## Terminology map

| Paper symbol | qModeling name |
|---|---|
| `K_s^(N)` (Eq. 9, per-root propagator factor) | the per-factor `(numerator, SparseLU)` pair in `qscat.evolution.pade.make_pade_stepper`'s `factors` list |
| `z_s^(N)` (Padé numerator roots, Eq. 7) | `qscat.evolution.pade._PADE_ROOTS[order]` — same underlying diagonal-Padé root set as van Dijk & Toyama's `z_s`, in a differing sign/labeling convention (this paper's `z_s` pairs with its own conjugate `[z_s]*` in one factor; `pade.py` instead stores each root with real part forced positive and builds numerator `(I - iHΔt/r)` / denominator `(I + iHΔt/r)` — algebraically equivalent, not transcribed literally; see `vandijk-2007-pre75-036707.md` for the root table this repo's values are checked against) |
| "generalized Crank-Nicholson method" | `qscat.evolution.make_pade_stepper` (order > 1); order 1 is plain Crank-Nicolson, `make_cn_stepper` |
| Chebyshev method (Eqs. 3-6) | not implemented anywhere in `qscat`; present only in `reference/eMoScat`'s decks as an alternative `approximation:` setting |

## Not used here

- The Chebyshev method itself (Eqs. 3-6: the Chebyshev-polynomial expansion of
  `exp(-iHΔt)`, the scaled-Hamiltonian operator `X`, coefficients `a_k` via Bessel
  functions `J_k`) — read for the comparison's other half only; `qscat` implements no
  Chebyshev propagator.
- The high-order finite-difference spatial discretization used for the test Hamiltonian
  (p. 668, prose above Table 1) — unrelated to `qscat.dvr`'s FEM-DVR-ECS spatial grid.
- Table 2's specific CPU-time/operation-count figures — the paper's own hardware-specific
  benchmark, not reused as a repo performance claim (`docs/physics/optimization-targets.md`
  and related notes use independently measured repo numbers).
- References [1]-[2], [6]-[8], [10] (review articles and other propagator methods) —
  background lineage only.
