# W. van Dijk, F. M. Toyama, Phys. Rev. E 75, 036707 (2007) — Accurate numerical solutions of the time-dependent Schrödinger equation

**Source:** `reference/literature/vandijk-2007-pre75-036707.pdf` (gitignored) · DOI 10.1103/PhysRevE.75.036707
**Pagination:** per-article, 036707-1 … 036707-10 (extractor page N = printed page 036707-N; offset zero, checked at page 1 and page 9).

## Why this repository cares

`qscat.evolution.make_pade_stepper` implements an order-N diagonal-Padé
propagator for `d/dt psi = -i H psi`, generalizing the Crank-Nicolson stepper
(order 1) to arbitrary order with claimed error `O(dt^(2N+1))` per step. This
paper's Sec. III is the origin of exactly that scheme: replacing
`exp(-iHΔt)` by the diagonal `[M,M]` Padé approximant, decomposed into a
product of `M` commuting complex-root factors (Eq. 3.3-3.7), each one sparse
mat-vec plus one back-substitution — the same construction `pade.py`'s
docstring and root table describe. Before this note, the repo cited no source
for the scheme (grep confirmed zero hits for "van Dijk"/"Toyama"/"Pade"
outside the code itself), exactly the gap Rescigno & McCurdy (2000) had for
FEM-DVR-ECS before that citation was added. The N₂ 2-D time-dependent
cross-section work (`docs/physics/n2-2d-td-cross-section.md`) records that
order-1 CN under-converged (~100% accumulated error) and that order-3 Padé
was required to reach ~1-2% agreement with the TI oracle — this paper is the
first place that order-M generalization, and its `O(Δt^(2M+1))` error
scaling, is derived.

**Important scope caveat:** this paper generalizes *two* things — the spatial
finite-difference stencil (Sec. II, an `r`-parametrized `(2r+1)`-point formula
giving `O(Δx^(2r-1))`) **and** the time-evolution operator (Sec. III, the
`M`-parametrized diagonal Padé approximant giving `O(Δt^(2M+1))`). qscat's
FEM-DVR-ECS spatial discretization (`qscat.dvr`) is unrelated to Sec. II's
finite-difference stencil; `make_pade_stepper` takes **only** the time-domain
piece (Sec. III). The repo's `order` parameter is this paper's `M`.

## Equations

```
Psi(x, t+dt) = exp(-i H dt / hbar) Psi(x, t)                              p. 036707-2, Eq. (2.3)
exp(-i H dt/hbar) = [1 - (1/2) i H dt/hbar] / [1 + (1/2) i H dt/hbar] + O(dt^3)   p. 036707-2, Eq. (2.4)
(1 + (1/2) i H dt/hbar) Psi(t+dt) = (1 - (1/2) i H dt/hbar) Psi(t)        p. 036707-2, Eq. (2.5)
f(z) = e^z = (sum_{m=0}^{M} a_m z^m) / (sum_{m'=0}^{M} b_{m'} z^{m'})     p. 036707-3, Eq. (3.1)
e^z = prod_{s=1}^{M} (1 - z/z_s^(M)) / (1 + z/zbar_s^(M)) + O(z^(2M+1))   p. 036707-4, Eq. (3.3)
K_s^(M) = [1 - (i H dt/hbar)/z_s^(M)] / [1 + (i H dt/hbar)/zbar_s^(M)]   p. 036707-4, Eq. (3.4)
exp(-i H dt/hbar) = prod_{s=1}^{M} K_s^(M) + O(dt^(2M+1))                p. 036707-4, Eq. (3.5)
Psi_{n+1} = prod_{s=1}^{M} K_s^(M) Psi_n   (factors commute, any order)  p. 036707-4, Eq. (3.6)-(3.7)
e^(r) = C^(r) (dx)^(2r)          [spatial truncation error, order r]     p. 036707-5, Eq. (4.1)
e^(M) = C^(M) (dt)^(2M+1)        [time truncation error, order M]       p. 036707-5, Eq. (4.6)
e^(M) ~ C^(M) (dt/M)^(2M+1)      [empirical M-scaled form]               p. 036707-5, Eq. (4.7)
```

`hbar` is written explicitly throughout (the paper does not use atomic
units); `libs/qscat/qscat/units.py`'s atomic-units convention sets `hbar=1`,
so qscat's `exp(-i H dt)` (no `hbar`) is this paper's `exp(-i H dt/hbar)` with
`hbar=1` — the same equation, unit convention resolved.

`qscat.evolution.pade._PADE_ROOTS` (`order=1..4`) matches Table II's

**positive** where the paper prints it negative — `pade.py`'s own comment
notes this is a deliberate sign choice (eMoScat's `Pade_Roots` stores orders
≥2 negated) verified independently by the code's own round-trip check
(`sum 1/r_i = 1/2`, product matches `exp(-z)` to the claimed order). The
higher-precision digits beyond the paper's 5 decimals were not sourced from
this PDF (the paper states it computed roots to 17-digit precision for `M`
up to 20 but tabulates only 5 decimals); `qscat`'s extra digits are not
checked against this paper's own higher-precision values, only against the
5-decimal table and against the code's independent numerical verification
described in its comment.

## Findings and limits

- The generalized scheme gives "circa ten or greater orders of magnitude"
  precision improvement and several orders of magnitude CPU-time reduction
  over plain CN, demonstrated up to `r=M=20` — p. 036707-1, abstract;
  p. 036707-9.
- Table IV's `M=r` sweep (`M=1..6`) shows the error dropping from `9.4e-2` at
  `M=1` to `8.4e-13` at `M=6`, empirically consistent with the
  `O(Δt^(2M+1))` scaling — p. 036707-7, Table IV.
- "It pays to increase `M` indefinitely" (no observed saturation in the
  time-evolution order), whereas the spatial order `r` has a
  problem-dependent optimum and saturates around `r≈10` — p. 036707-9,
  Sec. VI.
- The higher-order method requires well-behaved, sufficiently differentiable
  solutions/potentials; for singular potentials, higher order does not
  necessarily improve accuracy (citing Press et al., ref. [10]) —
  p. 036707-9.
- The scheme as derived applies only to a **time-independent** Hamiltonian;
  generalizing to explicitly time-dependent `H` at `M>1` is left as future
  work, citing Puzynin et al.'s `M=2` example (refs. [19],[20]) as the only
  precedent — p. 036707-9, Sec. VI. This matches `make_pade_stepper`'s
  signature, which takes one fixed sparse `H`.
- The unitarity of the diagonal Padé approximant of `exp(z)` (the property
  that motivates using this scheme for norm-preserving propagation) is
  stated as a known property of Padé approximants, cited to Baker &
  Graves-Morris (ref. [21]), not re-derived in this paper — p. 036707-3.
- No explicit stability bound or restriction on `Δt` is stated for any order;
  the scheme inherits CN's unconditional stability (constrained unitarity at
  every step) — p. 036707-1 – 036707-2, Sec. I.

## Not used here

- Sec. II's spatial finite-difference generalization (the `(2r+1)`-point
  stencil, coefficients `c_k^(r)` in Table I, and the resulting banded matrix
  equation Eq. (2.9)-(2.13)) — qscat's spatial grids come from FEM-DVR-ECS,
  not finite differences; this part of the paper is unrelated to any qscat
  code.
- Sec. IV.B's boundary-condition treatment for a radial (S-wave) coordinate
  with a discontinuous second derivative at the origin (the modified matrix
  `A' = A − B`, Eq. (4.9)) — qscat's ECS complex-tail boundary handling is a
  different mechanism (`qscat.ecs.ecs_map`), not this paper's ansatz.
- Sec. V's three worked examples (coherent-state oscillation in a harmonic
  well, wave-packet scattering off a Gaussian barrier, and long-time decay of
  a quasistable δ-shell-like state) and Figs. 1-5/Tables III-IV in full — read
  for the qualitative `O(Δt^(2M+1))` scaling evidence only (Table IV, used
  above); no other numeric values from these examples are used.
- The discussion of extending the method to time-dependent Hamiltonians, to
  2-D/3-D systems via a Peaceman-Rachford/ADI-type factorization, and to
  variable spatial step size (Sec. VI, forward-looking remarks) — none of
  this is implemented in qscat; `make_pade_stepper` already handles qscat's
  actual N-D case by operating on the full assembled sparse `H` (via
  `qscat.dvr.hamiltonian_nd`), not through an ADI-type dimensional split.
